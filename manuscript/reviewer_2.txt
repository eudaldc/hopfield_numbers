The manuscript proposes an original computational model of numerosity perception using a 1D Hopfield attractor network with Poisson-shaped wells anchored at integers. It targets the subitizing and estimation regimes (more indirectly the density regimen) and fits three empirical measures (mean estimate, exact accuracy, RT) across five exposure durations made available by Cheyette and Piantadosi (2020).

Overall, I enjoyed reading the manuscript and found it stimulating. I think that framing numerosity judgement as pattern completion in an attractor landscape is an original idea (but see my point #1 below). The proposed model simultaneously fits estimation, accuracy, and RTs (which is more demanding than single-measure approaches) and the reported R² values are generally strong (but see my point #2 below).

At the same time, I have some major concerns that should be addressed to make the manuscript stronger:
1) The Author properly positions the work with respect to relevant literature, but it should be acknowledged that others have recently tried to model numerosity perception using Hopfield-like approaches:
Yago Malo, J., Cicchini, G. M., Morrone, M. C., & Chiofalo, M. L. (2023). Quantum spin models for numerosity perception. PLoS One, 18(4), e0284610.
2) AIC differences are modest: The Hopfield–Poisson advantage over competitors is relatively small, especially in terms of Global fit. Given the parameter trade-offs, this also makes it is unclear how robustly the attractor mechanism — rather than the Poisson family — drives the performance improvement.
3) Parameter identifiability: The recovery analysis reveals a strong trade-off between c and κ (ρ = −0.87) and between s₀ and λ₀ (ρ = −0.68). While the Author acknowledges this, it weakens causal interpretation of individual parameters, particularly across the developmental dyscalculia extension. This could be better highlighted.
4) It is also not totally clear to me why the model parameters should be fitted separately across exposure durations; is the Author suggesting that the specific mechanism for judging the stimuli differs depending on the timing of the stimulation? This should be discussed more explicitly.
5) As also acknowledged by the Author, the proposed model completely lacks a perceptual module, which would allow simulating key phenomena related to numerosity perception, such as the role of continuous magnitudes. This limitation should be discussed more in depth, also considering that alternative modeling frameworks indeed allow to account for such phenomena (Testolin et al., 2020) and have recently been used also to simulate dyscalculic profiles (Dolfi et al., 2024).
Testolin, A., Dolfi, S., Rochus, M., & Zorzi, M. (2020). Visual sense of number vs. sense of magnitude in humans and machines. Scientific reports, 10(1), 10045.
Dolfi, S., Decarli, G., Lunardon, M., De Filippo De Grazia, M., Gerola, S., Lanfranchi, S., ... & Zorzi, M. (2024). Weaker number sense accounts for impaired numerosity perception in dyscalculia: Behavioral and computational evidence. Developmental Science, 27(6), e13538.
