\title{An attractor account of numerosity perception}

\author{Eudald Correig-Fraga\affilnum{1}}

\affiliation{%

\begin{abstract}
Numerical cognition is classically split between two systems: an exact mechanism for small sets and an approximate one for larger ones, separated by a poorly understood boundary. We propose a mechanistic alternative: the integers act like memories on the number line, each an attractor well that widens with magnitude, so that numerosity judgement is pattern completion of a noisy estimate. Fitted to individual brief-exposure responses, the model outperforms non-attractor and Weber-style alternatives; its dynamics improve the fit without adding parameters and predict response times from convergence time and residual uncertainty. The model predicts its own limit: the numerosity where neighbouring wells stop being resolvable, which grows with exposure and marks the response-time plateau. A single core number system, the full set of integer attractors, is read in two modes: exact where the wells resolve (the Exact Number System) and approximate where they merge (the Approximate Number System).

\end{abstract}

\keywords{numerical cognition, subitizing, exact number system, attractor network, approximate number system}

\section{Introduction}
%% ====================================================================

%% Background

% There is OTS and ANS, and nothing in between
Humans perceive small numerosities (1--3/4) quickly and accurately, a phenomenon known as subitizing~\citep{kaufman_discrimination_1949}. Beyond this range, response time increases and accuracy deteriorates, with variability that grows with set size in the ratio-dependent manner characteristic of Weber's law~\citep{mandler_subitizing_1982, dehaene_number_2011}. These two signatures have been documented across ages, cultures, and species~\citep{feigenson_core_2004, pica_exact_2004, nieder_neuronal_2016}, and they anchor the standard view of numerical cognition as the product of two core systems: an Object Tracking System (OTS) that individuates a handful of items exactly, and an Approximate Number System (ANS) that represents larger magnitudes noisily~\citep{piazza_subitizing_2011, feigenson_representations_2002, halberda_individual_2008}. Nevertheless, the mechanisms underlying each system, and the transitions between them, remain debated.

% Computational work has shown that it might be only one mechanism
Computational work has shown that the two regimes need not reflect two mechanisms.~\citet{cheyette_unified_2020} offer the clearest normative formulation: under a power-law prior and a finite information-processing constraint, near-exact performance for small numerosities and Weber-like variability for larger ones emerge from a single optimization.~\citet{cheyette_limited_2024} later pushed the argument upstream, proposing that the psychophysics of number follows from bandwidth limits on visuospatial memory rather than from number-specific representations, and~\citet{malo_quantum_2023} showed that a small quantum spin model can encode numerosity in the spectrum of its collective dynamics. Recurrent networks with biologically grounded on-centre off-surround dynamics likewise produce a partition of the numerosity range from a single architecture~\citep{sengupta_visual_2014}, and image-computable networks account for how numerosity is extracted from the visual scene in the first place~\citep{stoianov_emergence_2012, testolin_visual_2020}. 

% This visual mechanisms do not explain the numerical code in non-visual tasks, or accounts of cross-model adaptation (Burr), or accounts of accuity related to math knowledge, or IPS causal studies
These accounts treat numerosity as a product of the visual system, whether as a bandwidth limit on visuospatial memory or as a quantity read off the image. Number, however, behaves as an abstract quantity that is not bound to any one sense. It transfers across modalities, so that adapting to a stream of sounds or touches shifts the apparent numerosity of a visual array~\citep{arrighi_generalized_2014}, its acuity tracks numerical experience, sharpening with formal schooling in otherwise comparable adults~\citep{piazza_education_2013, nys_does_2013}, and it depends causally on parietal cortex, where stimulating the intraparietal sulcus disrupts numerosity while sparing lower-level vision~\citep{cappelletti_rtms_2007, dormal_contribution_2012} and focal damage yields acalculias that extend even to enumerating touches~\citep{ashkenazi_basic_2008, cohen_tactile_2018}. This picture is not uniform: nonsymbolic number stays robust with little formal schooling once memory and symbolic demands are minimised~\citep{frank_number_2008, zebian_differences_2012, pica_exact_2004}, and clean parietal lesions with intact working memory are scarce. Even so, these visual-limit accounts capture a real part of the psychophysics but leave untouched a representation of quantity that is amodal, shaped by experience, and parietally instantiated.


% Alternative account of numerosity coding that is modality agnostic and accounts for both accumulation/summation and neural coding of number
Classic models of numerosity coding separate two stages: a summation stage that accumulates sensory activity into a magnitude, and a set of number-tuned units that label it, as in the summation and numerosity clusters of the detector model of~\citet{dehaene_development_1993}. Here we propose a single, modality-agnostic mechanism in which both arise from one process. The mental number line is cast as a one-dimensional attractor landscape in the sense of modern Hopfield networks~\citep{hopfield_neural_1982, ramsauer_hopfield_2021}: each integer is a stored memory, a well whose width grows with magnitude, parameterised by how often numbers occur in the environment~\citep{dehaene_cross-linguistic_1992}. Numerosity judgement is pattern completion, a noisy sensory estimate relaxing to the nearest resolvable well, so that pooling across the stored integers and the discrete number readout fall out of the same dynamics. The mechanism also gives a natural account of response time, set jointly by the clean-up dynamics, which depend on the well width, and by the residual readout entropy, the latency attributed to remaining uncertainty.

On this view, the single sequence of integer attractors is read in two modes. When the wells resolve, pattern completion recovers the exact numerosity; because this exactness is a property of the number representation itself rather than of a general system for individuating and tracking objects~\citep{feigenson_representations_2002}, we treat it as a purely numerical Exact Number System (ENS)~\citep{butterworth_investigating_2017} rather than a by-product of object tracking. Beyond $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ the wells merge and the same readout returns a coarse, ratio-dependent estimate, the Approximate Number System (ANS). Both are readings of one landscape, so a single mechanism spans the exact and approximate regimes rather than handing off between independent modules.

Finally, the same landscape also predicts its own limit: the critical numerosity $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$, where neighbouring wells stop being resolvable, is not stipulated but read off the fitted widths; it grows with exposure duration and coincides with the onset of the empirical response-time plateau.
% FALTA: ampliar o ajuntar amb algun altre

We evaluate the model on ${\sim}$53,000 individual responses from the brief-exposure dataset of~\citet{cheyette_unified_2020}. It outperforms Weber/ANS and normative alternatives; its dynamics sharpen the fit without adding parameters, predict response times within the exact range from the settling time and the residual uncertainty, and place $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ where the response-time plateau begins.

\section{Methods}
%% ====================================================================

The model maps a noisy perceptual estimate of numerosity onto a discrete response through relaxation on an energy landscape whose stable states sit at the integers (Fig.~\ref{fig:conceptual}a). A single trial has three stages: a sensory estimate provides a starting point, gradient descent cleans it up toward a well, and a probability distribution over integers is read out from the resting state. The same dynamics also determine how long the trial takes.

Clean-up removes the residual uncertainty of the small-$n$ readout at the cost of a few settling steps; near the boundary the settling time grows and the residual entropy rises. \textbf{c}, The resulting response time, $\mathrm{RT} = t_0 + \tau\,E[T] + \kappa\,E[H]$, decomposed into its basal, convergence-time, and readout-uncertainty components.}

\paragraph{Attractor landscape.} Each integer $n$ is a stored memory represented by a Gaussian well centred at $n$, with a width that grows with magnitude. We tie the width to how often each number occurs in the environment: number-word frequency follows an approximately power-law (Benford-like) decline across languages~\citep{dehaene_cross-linguistic_1992}, so frequent small numbers occupy narrow, stable basins and rare large numbers occupy broad ones. Writing $f_n = \log_{10}(1 + 1/n)$ for the frequency of $n$, the well width is
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:width}
\sigma_n = \left(\sigma_{c} / f_n\right)^{\beta},
\end{equation}
%
where $\sigma_c$ sets the overall scale and the exponent $\beta$ controls how strongly frequency modulates width. We leave $\beta$ free because there is no a priori reason for the mapping from frequency to width to be linear; as we show below, the data require it to be super-linear ($\beta \approx 2$), which is what makes small numbers sharply resolved and large ones broadly overlapping. Each well contributes a log-likelihood
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:well}
g_n(x) = -\log\sigma_n - \frac{(x - n)^2}{2\,\sigma_n^2},
\end{equation}
%
and, following modern Hopfield networks~\citep{ramsauer_hopfield_2021}, the attraction potential is the log-sum-exp over all wells, $V(x) = -\log \sum_n \exp\!\big(g_n(x)\big)$.

\paragraph{Clean-up and readout.} A sensory estimate $y_0$ initialises the state; we model the sensory channel as $y_0 \sim \mathcal{N}\!\big(n,\; s_{00}^2 + s_{0}\,n^{\lambda_0}\big)$, a neutral front end whose noise grows with numerosity ($s_{00}$ fixed, $s_0$ and $\lambda_0$ fitted). From $x = y_0$ the state descends the potential, $x_{t+1} = x_t - \eta\,\partial V/\partial x$. Because $V$ is a log-sum-exp over the stored integers, each step moves the state toward a softmax combination of the wells, weighted by their precisions, so every stored integer contributes to the final position. The descent runs until the state settles or begins to oscillate, giving a resting state $x^*$ reached in $T$ steps. A distribution over integers is then read out, $p(n \mid x^*) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\big(g_n(x^*)\big)$, from which we take the mean estimate and the most probable integer (exact accuracy).

\paragraph{Response time.} The chronometric prediction combines the two quantities the mechanism itself produces: the time the state takes to settle and the uncertainty that remains once it has,
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt}
\mathrm{RT} = t_{0} + \tau\,\mathbb{E}[T] + \kappa\,\mathbb{E}\big[H(p(n \mid x^*))\big],
\end{equation}
%
where $T$ is the number of clean-up steps, $H = -\sum_n p \log p$ is the residual readout entropy, and expectations are taken over the sensory noise. The convergence-time term $\tau\,\mathbb{E}[T]$ captures the deliberation the attractor dynamics require (long near the boundary, where the landscape flattens and the state settles slowly), while $\kappa\,\mathbb{E}[H]$ captures how undecided the readout remains between neighbouring integers. We compare this hybrid link against its two nested special cases (entropy only, $\tau=0$; steps only, $\kappa=0$). The basal latency $t_0$ is task-specific and fixed per dataset.

\paragraph{Trial-level likelihood.} We fit the model to the individual responses of~\citet{cheyette_unified_2020}, in which adult participants ($N = 100$ per condition) briefly viewed dot arrays of $1$--$15$ items and typed an estimate, at five exposure durations (40, 80, 160, 320, 640~ms). Rather than fitting summary curves, we evaluate the categorical likelihood of the ${\sim}53{,}000$ individual integer reports. For each numerosity the response probability marginalises the readout over the sensory noise,
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal}
p(r \mid n) = \int p\big(r \mid x^*(y_0)\big)\,\phi\!\big(y_0; n, \sigma(n)\big)\,dy_0,
\end{equation}
%
computed by deterministic grid quadrature (Supplementary Material 1). Typed responses run well beyond the well support, so we add a uniform lapse channel, $p_{\mathrm{final}}(r \mid n) = (1-\ell)\,p(r \mid n) + \ell\,/\,R_{\max}$, with $\ell$ fitted per duration and counted in all information criteria. The response model therefore has four mechanism parameters ($\sigma_c$, $s_0$, $\lambda_0$, $\beta$) plus the lapse, fitted by differential evolution~\citep{storn_differential_1997} and Nelder--Mead polishing both separately per duration and, as a stricter test, with the landscape shared across durations (Supplementary Material 2); the RT channel adds $\tau$ and $\kappa$. The response, RT, and coupled likelihoods are optimised as described in Supplementary Material 1.

\paragraph{Model comparison.} To isolate the contribution of the attractor dynamics, we compare the model against a graded set of alternatives fitted under the identical trial-level convention: static (no-clean-up) readouts of the same wells, a Weber/ANS code with magnitude-proportional noise, and a log-prior normative proxy in the spirit of~\citet{cheyette_unified_2020}. Because turning clean-up on or off leaves the parameter count unchanged, the attractor--vs--static contrast is a zero-parameter comparison. Supplementary Material 2 gives the full ladder and the role of the width exponent. Model quality is summarised by AIC and BIC over the full response count table.

\paragraph{Resolution limit.} Finally, the landscape yields a parameter-free prediction of where the exact code breaks down. Adjacent wells stop being reliably distinguishable when their separation falls to one standard deviation, $d' = \text{spacing}/\sigma_n = 1$~\citep{macmillan_detection_2004}; solving $\sigma_n = 1$ gives the critical numerosity $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ at which the discrete code loses resolution. This quantity is derived from the fitted landscape, not from any boundary in the data.

\paragraph{Developmental extension.} As an exploratory application, we fit the same model to the exact-enumeration data of~\citet{decarli_impaired_2020} from children with and without developmental dyscalculia, localising the group difference within the landscape (Supplementary Material 5).

\section{Results}
%% ====================================================================

We fitted the model to the ${\sim}53{,}000$ individual responses in two ways: a joint fit, in which a single landscape is shared across all five exposure durations and only the sensory channel and lapse are free per duration, and a separate fit per duration (Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}a,b; full ladder in Supplementary Material 2). Under both, the attractor is the best account of the data: with one shared landscape it beats the fully free per-duration Weber/ANS code by $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} \approx 900$ and the normative log-prior proxy by $\approx 4100$, and with separate fits the margins widen to $\approx 4400$ and $\approx 7700$. The width exponent, fitted freely, lands firmly super-linear ($\beta \approx 2$), so the wells come out sharp for small numbers and broadly overlapping for large ones.

Switching the clean-up dynamics off while holding everything else fixed leaves a static readout of the same wells with the same number of parameters, so the contrast between the two isolates what the dynamics themselves contribute. The static readout fits worse, by $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} \approx 780$ under the shared landscape and $\approx 1260$ with separate fits: the dynamics sharpen the response distribution without adding flexibility. Their contribution is concentrated at the briefest exposures, where the sensory evidence is poorest; at 320--640~ms the static readout does slightly better on the response distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}b), as expected if the attractor has largely settled during the exposure itself, leaving little for the post-stimulus dynamics to resolve.

With these ingredients the model reproduces all three behavioural signatures together (Fig.~\ref{fig:fit}): pooled across durations, $R^2 = 0.99$ for the mean estimate, $0.94$ for exact accuracy, and $0.88$ for response time, the last a genuine cross-prediction of a channel fitted to the individual trial RTs rather than to a summary curve. The one systematic miss is a mild under-prediction of exact accuracy in the subitizing band ($n = 2$--$4$): the model captures the trend toward near-perfect small-number performance but not its full sharpness, which we return to in the Discussion.

The small shoulder in the predicted exact accuracy near the resolution limit $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is a signature of the discrete clean-up basins; it disappears when the clean-up is switched off (Supplementary Material 4). $R^2$ values are given per panel.}

Because clean-up is a dynamical process, response time can be derived from how long it runs rather than from a static uncertainty proxy, and the dynamical link is the better one (Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}c,d): a link based on the attractor's settling time alone already outperforms the entropy proxy by $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} \approx 540$, and the hybrid link that adds the residual entropy is best (${\approx}770$ better than entropy, ${\approx}230$ better than steps alone). The same ordering holds when a single RT mapping is shared across durations (Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}c; the hybrid link then beats the entropy proxy by ${\approx}950$) and when the response and response-time channels are fitted together as a single coupled likelihood (Supplementary Material 2). The two terms play complementary roles (Fig.~\ref{fig:conceptual}b--c): near the boundary the landscape flattens and the state settles slowly, so the convergence-time term drives most of the rise in RT, while the residual-entropy term dominates once the wells have merged. The chronometric prediction is thus not an add-on but a second readout of the same mechanism.

The model also predicts its own resolution limit. Because the well width grows with numerosity, the landscape specifies where the discrete code must fail: the numerosity $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ at which adjacent wells reach $\sigma_n = 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}e). This crossing falls \emph{inside} the fitted range, at $n_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 4.6,\,7.5,\,8.7,\,9.2,\,10.0$ for the five durations; it is not imposed by any cutoff, since the model is fitted on the full range. Two independent facts converge on it. First, $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ grows monotonically with exposure: more viewing time sharpens the wells, so the exact range extends from ${\sim}5$ at a 40~ms flash to ${\sim}10$ at 640~ms. This duration dependence is a property of the landscape: the wells sharpen as the sensory evidence improves, reflecting what a trainable memory should show (a constrained duration-independent landscape is examined in Supplementary Material 2). Second, the response-time plateau in the data begins near $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$: within the exact range the model tracks RT closely, and above it the deliberation-time prediction keeps rising while the empirical RT flattens (Fig.~\ref{fig:conceptual}c and Supplementary Material 4), the point at which participants stop resolving and start approximating. Accuracy and chronometry, two independent channels, thus mark the same boundary.

A parameter-recovery analysis (Supplementary Material 3) confirms that the quantities we interpret are identifiable at the trial level: the width exponent $\beta$ and the derived $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ are recovered accurately ($R^2 = 0.89$ and $0.996$), while the two sensory parameters trade off and are interpreted only through the combined noise profile $\sigma(n)$. A brief exploratory application to developmental dyscalculia, where the same model localises the deficit as a shorter exact range, is reported in Supplementary Material 5.

\section{Discussion}
%% ====================================================================

We have described numerosity perception as pattern completion on an attractor landscape: the integers act like memories whose widths grow with magnitude, and a noisy estimate relaxes to the nearest resolvable well. Fitted to individual responses, the model reproduces estimation, accuracy, and response time together, and, unlike accounts that stipulate a subitizing capacity, it derives the limit of exact perception from the structure of the representation itself. The exact and approximate regimes become two readings of one landscape rather than two systems joined at an unexplained seam.

Two features of the account carry the explanatory weight. The first is the clean-up dynamics. Turning clean-up on is a zero-parameter change, yet it improves the fit substantially, and the attractor's convergence time predicts response time better than a static uncertainty proxy. The clean-up is associative retrieval in the modern Hopfield sense: each step moves the state toward a softmax-weighted average of the stored integers, so every well shapes the outcome. Because those patterns are the ordered points of the number line, not arbitrary memories, the softmax competition among neighbours is sharp for small, well-separated numbers and broad for large, overlapping ones. The graded loss of resolution and the lengthening of settling time toward the boundary both follow from this structure. The second feature is that the well widths are set by number frequency. This ties the representation to the broader view of numbers as memory traces whose strength reflects experience~\citep{anderson_reflections_1991, verguts_representation_2004}: frequent small numbers are sharply encoded, rare large ones diffusely.

Casting the code as a frequency-shaped memory has a developmental consequence that a fixed-capacity account lacks. The resolution limit $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is not a constant but a property of how sharp the wells are, and we already see it move with exposure over milliseconds; over development it should move with numerical experience. As children encounter number words and counting, the wells that carry the most exposure should sharpen, extending the exact range and pushing the transition to approximate perception upward. This predicts that the boundary sits at very small numbers early in development and grows with numeracy, and, more specifically, that it should be trained by exposure to \emph{numbers}, not by general improvement in visual acuity: an intervention that sharpens the number code should differ in its signature from one that improves perception at large~\citep{butterworth_investigating_2017}. The developmental dyscalculia analysis (Supplementary Material 5) is an early step in this direction: the same model localises the deficit not as noisier perception but as a shorter exact range, a code whose resolution runs out earlier.

The approximate regime is the same landscape read where the wells have merged. Beyond $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ clean-up no longer sharpens the posterior, and the same readout returns a coarse, ratio-dependent estimate, the behaviour attributed to the Approximate Number System. This needs no separate module: the model's response distributions match the data across the whole range, and their coefficient of variation settles to a roughly constant plateau across the estimation range, matching the Weber fraction measured in numerosity estimation~\citep{testolin_estimates_2021} (Supplementary Material 4). One mechanism therefore accounts for numerosity estimation in both regimes, exact where the wells resolve and Weber-law approximate where they merge.

The model's clearest failure is chronometric. Within the exact range the convergence-time term tracks response time, but beyond $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ it keeps rising while the empirical RT plateaus (Fig.~\ref{fig:conceptual}c). The model runs clean-up to convergence at every numerosity; participants appear instead to stop deliberating once the code is no longer resolvable, reading the merged posterior directly and answering quickly. Some mechanism halts the clean-up around $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and flattens the response time, and what triggers it is unknown. Because stopping early and running to convergence leave a similarly broad readout on merged wells, this affects the latency but not the estimate, which is why the model captures the approximate estimates while over-predicting how long they take. Whether numerosity adaptation involves the same halting is a natural question for future work.

This account offers a different level of explanation from the leading unifying alternative. \citet{cheyette_limited_2024} derive number psychophysics from a bandwidth limit on visuospatial memory; in our model that limit would live in the sensory channel $\sigma(n)$, upstream of the attractor, and the observation that $n_{\mathrm{crit}}$ grows with exposure is consistent with more available bandwidth yielding a finer code. The accounts are therefore complementary rather than rival, and both invoke a frequency prior of the Benford family. Where they differ is in what the number-specific representation contributes: our attractor is modality-general (any channel delivering a scalar estimate feeds the same wells) and, being a memory, is intrinsically trainable. It also differs from recurrent accounts that partition the numerosity range through fixed network dynamics~\citep{sengupta_visual_2014}: our boundary is not a fixed partition but one that moves with exposure and, we predict, with development, a discriminating test between the two.

Several limitations remain. The evidence channel is deliberately neutral: it summarises the visual front end as a noise profile $\sigma(n)$, so the model operates on an already-extracted estimate. This isolates the attractor dynamics but, by construction, sets aside the influence of continuous magnitudes (area, density, convex hull) on numerosity judgements; coupling the wells to an image-computable front end that is itself sensitive to number~\citep{stoianov_emergence_2012, testolin_visual_2020}, and that has been used to simulate dyscalculic profiles~\citep{dolfi_weaker_2024}, is the clearest way to close this gap. The model also under-predicts the sharpness of small-number accuracy at $n = 2$--$4$: it captures the trend but not the full abruptness of the subitizing edge, which may still require a fast parallel individuation process for the very smallest sets~\citep{feigenson_representations_2002}. Finally, the model is specified at a computational and algorithmic level; relating the wells to the number-tuned populations of parietal cortex, whose tuning widths grow with preferred numerosity~\citep{piazza_tuning_2004, harvey_topographic_2013}, is a natural direction for testing it neurally.

Numerical cognition, in this framework, is pattern completion on a landscape of number memories. A single set of wells, read exactly where they are resolved and approximately where they merge, accounts for the fast exactness of small numbers, the graded approximation of larger ones, the response times of both, and the moving boundary between them. The result is a compact, mechanistic set of hypotheses about how the mind represents number.
